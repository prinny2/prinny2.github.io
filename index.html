LeadBellus

leadbellus.com.br, source on GitHub

Aesthetic clinics in Brazil sell over WhatsApp. LeadBellus takes a client's message, like "how much is Botox?" or "that's too expensive", and writes three replies in the clinic's tone. Someone on the team picks one and sends it. You can try it on the home page without signing up.

What I built

  • The whole app, alone: Next.js 16, React 19, TypeScript and Firestore.
  • A fallback chain across OpenAI, Claude and Gemini with timeouts and retries, so one provider going down doesn't stop the product.
  • A check after generation that flags what a clinic must not say, like guaranteed results, diagnoses or fixed prices. It still misses things, so I keep adding rules. The latest ones reject replies where the model made up a schedule, a duration or a clinic policy.
  • Stripe subscriptions in live mode, with checkout, a billing portal and idempotent webhooks. Free usage is limited inside a Firestore transaction, so concurrent requests can't get around it.
  • A WhatsApp integration through Z-API with an inbox and auto-reply. It's built, but switched off on the live product, which works by copy and paste so clinics stay in control of what gets sent.
  • 400+ Vitest tests with every network call mocked, and a demo mode that runs the whole app with no API keys.

Where it stands: live at R$97 a month, and pre-revenue. The software works. Getting clinics to try it is the hard part, and that's what I'm working on now, with landing page changes, Instagram content and Meta ads.

ResonAnza

resonanza.com.br, code in a private repo

Brazil's oil regulator publishes records for 31,244 wells: depths, temperatures, logs and production. Decades of that data were collected to find oil. ResonAnza reads it again to find energy, mainly heat.

What I built

  • A Python pipeline that reads scanned well reports with Claude, corrects bottom-hole temperatures with the Horner method and calibrates per basin.
  • A FastAPI service, a Typer CLI and a React 19 dashboard, with exports to PDF reports, GeoJSON and KML. It runs on SQLite locally and on Cloud Run with Postgres in production.
  • 450+ pytest tests, with lint and coverage in CI.
  • Every number is labeled as measured or modeled and keeps its source. A missing value stays missing instead of becoming a zero that looks like a measurement.

The part I'd tell you about over coffee

The first plan was to generate electricity from hot wells. Then I pulled temperatures from real well logs, and onshore gradients came back at 24 to 29 °C/km. That's too low for the turbines the plan depended on, so I dropped it. The focus now is direct heat: three mature fields in the Potiguar Basin produce about 107,000 m³ of water a day, and that water carries heat a small module could recover.

Where it stands: a registered company in Brazil at the study stage. No paying customer or pilot yet.
